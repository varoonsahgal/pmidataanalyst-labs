Theme · Session 2 of 4 Today's theme

Deepfake data

Generated faces are built to look real, and people trust them. Generated data is built the same way: to look like your consumers. It will have the right column names and believable percentages. Whether it behaves like the real population is a separate question, and only the warehouse can answer it.

Realistic is not real.
Play it · 2 minutesWhich Face Is Real?Two faces, one generated. Pick the real one, five rounds in a row. University of Washington, Calling Bullshit project.

The research

In a 2022 study, people picked the real face from generated ones 48.2% of the time: worse than a coin toss. And they rated the generated faces 7.7% more trustworthy (Nightingale & Farid, PNAS). Realistic doesn't just pass. It persuades.

One more idea runs through this hour

Premium planning, thrifty execution

The same vendor sells the model in sizes. The expensive one buys judgment, not fluency: every tier types SQL and tables fine. So spend the expensive model where the thinking is, and the cheap one where the typing is.

Check A · Which age bands exist?

How many age bands does HORIZON use, and what's the top band? SELECT age_band, min_age, max_age FROM ref.age_bands ORDER BY min_age;
5 bands18-24 · 25-34 · 35-44 · 45-54 · 55+There is no 55-64 and no 65+.

If the blueprint invented bands, every dashboard filter built on the synthetic cohort would break the day real data arrives.

Check B · Who may we market to, by age band?

What share of Japan's 18-24 consent records allow marketing? (In Italy and Indonesia it is 0%.) SELECT c.age_band, round(100.0 * avg(m.marketing_allowed::int), 1) AS pct_consent_rows_allowed FROM clr.consumers c JOIN mpl.consent_records m USING (consumer_id) WHERE c.market_code = 'JP' GROUP BY c.age_band ORDER BY c.age_band;

In Japan the under-25 band is not restricted. A blueprint that set 18-24 to 0% ("under-25 means no marketing") over-corrected and erased a legitimate audience. One that set it to 100% fabricated permission. The same assumption can be right in Italy and wrong in Japan.

Unit note: this is the share of consent records that allow marketing (Japan 18-24: 675 of 924). For a one-row-per-consumer prototype we use it as the probability that a consumer is marketing-allowed, and we write that down as an assumption.

Check C · How do real Japan ZYN consumers behave?

What share of Japan ZYN consumers reached trial or conversion? WITH zyn AS ( SELECT c.consumer_id, c.age_band, bool_or(e.lifecycle_stage IN ('trial', 'conversion')) AS adopted, bool_or(e.lifecycle_stage = 'conversion') AS converted, bool_or(e.lifecycle_stage = 'retention') AS retained FROM clr.consumers c JOIN clr.lifecycle_events e ON e.consumer_id = c.consumer_id AND e.product_code = 'ZYN' WHERE c.market_code = 'JP' GROUP BY c.consumer_id, c.age_band ) SELECT coalesce(age_band, 'ALL') AS age_band, count(*) AS consumers, round(100.0 * count(*) / (SELECT count(*) FROM zyn), 1) AS pct_of_cohort, round(100.0 * avg(adopted::int), 1) AS pct_adopted, round(100.0 * avg(converted::int), 1) AS pct_converted, round(100.0 * sum(retained::int) / nullif(sum(converted::int), 0), 1) AS pct_retained_of_converted FROM zyn GROUP BY ROLLUP (age_band) ORDER BY age_band;

What this query does: for each real Japan consumer with ZYN events, it flags whether they ever reached trial/conversion, conversion, and retention (bool_or = "true if any of their events matched"). Then it summarises per age band, and ROLLUP adds an "ALL" row for the whole cohort.

Check D · Does ZYN have any engagement data?

How many ZYN rows are in Japan's device telemetry table? SELECT product_code, count(*) AS telemetry_rows FROM det.device_telemetry WHERE market_code = 'JP' GROUP BY product_code ORDER BY product_code;

No ZYN row. ZYN is a pouch: no device, no telemetry. If the blueprint added a "device sessions" or "usage minutes" column for ZYN, it fabricated a structure that does not and cannot exist. (ref.products agrees: ZYN has_device = false.)

If your blueprint said "no device signal for a pouch; use purchase frequency instead", good. It still needed the warehouse to sign it. "The model said so" is not evidence.

Tokens, not tallies

What often happens: the totals are close but off by a row or two, or the profile Claude printed doesn't match the rows it actually wrote. A model can't reliably count its own output, so its summary of the data it made is another claim to check, not a fact. The warehouse counted. You signed.

Key takeaways

Realistic is not real.

Generated cohorts, scenarios and segmentations are built to look plausible. Prove their structure (bands, consent share, rates, which products even have data) against real aggregates before anyone builds on them. Where the output diverges from the warehouse, the output is wrong.

Premium planning, thrifty execution.

Spend the expensive model on the short, judgment-heavy plan; prove the plan; let the cheap model do the long, mechanical typing; then count what it produced. The deliverable is a documented asset (grain, assumptions, provenance, owner), not a chat message.
