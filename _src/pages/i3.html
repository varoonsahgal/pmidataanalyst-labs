Understand it · 1 minute

The point

Every session today has one sentence at its centre. This is Session 3's. Every section below ends with a "Back to the point" box that ties what you just read back to it.

The sentence the session proves

Every "up 20%" or "ahead of plan" is secretly a comparison. If you can't say what it's compared to, it isn't a finding yet.

So before anyone signs a number, they name three things the sentence never says: the baseline (compared to what?), the window (over which dates?) and the population (about whom?). Then they check each one in the data.

Here's what happens in the lab, in plain words. An AI-generated sentence appears on a dashboard: "VEEV launch adoption in Indonesia is tracking 20% ahead of plan." It sounds finished. Students first watch Claude happily explain why it's ahead, then just as happily change its story when they push back. Then they go looking for the evidence the sentence depends on, and find that it isn't there:

0 rowsStep 2 · the baseline

"Ahead of plan" needs a plan. No table in the warehouse holds one.

0 / 116Step 3 · the comparison

"Ahead because of the campaign" needs campaign-driven conversions. None of 116 is tagged to a campaign.

1/5Step 4 · the finding

"Not yet verifiable", with what's missing and who must fix it. That is the answer.

The twist students don't expect: the claim isn't proven wrong. It's untestable with this data. Learning to say "we can't tell yet, here's why, and here's who has to fix what" is the skill.

The day's rule in this session: AI answers. Data decides. You sign. Here the AI answered (fluently, twice, in opposite directions); the data couldn't decide; so the honest thing to sign is "not yet".

If you remember one thing: a number with nothing on the other side of the comparison always "wins", which is exactly why you can't trust it. No baseline, no claim.
Understand it · 3 minutes

The story, and the business words

The lab is a small workplace story in four panels. First the panels, then every company and marketing word in them, decoded.

The claim

An AI-generated insight lands on the Indonesia dashboard: "VEEV launch adoption is tracking 20% ahead of plan."

The shortcut

The analyst asks Claude to explain what's driving it. Claude writes three confident sentences. Pushed back, it writes the opposite story.

The catch

"Ahead of plan" needs a plan; "the campaign worked" needs a campaign group. The warehouse has neither.

The fix

Rewrite the claim as a testable question, test it, and publish a confidence-rated finding: "not yet verifiable", plus who must fix what.

The business words, decoded

PMI · VEEV · Indonesia the setting

PMI (Philip Morris International) sells "smoke-free" products. VEEV is its e-cigarette. Indonesia (market code ID) is a new, small market for it: PMI really launched VEEV there in August 2026.

Same market and product as Session 1. Small numbers: 116 VEEV conversions over two years.

Claim a sentence that sounds like a fact

A statement someone (or some AI) puts on a slide or dashboard: "up 20%", "ahead of plan", "the campaign lifted sales". It sounds finished. It hasn't been checked.

"VEEV launch adoption in Indonesia is tracking 20% ahead of plan."

Plan · target what we expected

The number the business hoped for, usually set in a budget or forecast. "Ahead of plan" means "higher than that expected number". To check it, you need to see the plan.

There is no plan anywhere in HORIZON. Step 2 proves it.

Adoption · conversion the metric

Two stages of a customer's journey: registration (signs up) → trial (tries it) → conversion (switches for real) → retention or churn. "Adoption" here loosely means people taking up VEEV; the lab measures conversions.

a gym: a free trial class is a trial; buying the membership is a conversion.

Conversion rate a share, not a count

Out of a group, the percentage who converted. A count says "116 people"; a rate says "13.5% of them". Rates are what "lifts" are usually measured on.

Only in the Italy case file: 13.5% before a campaign, 21.4% after.

Campaign a marketing push

A planned burst of marketing (emails, posts, shop promotions) with a start and end date and a channel. HORIZON lists five in ref.campaigns, three of them in Indonesia.

Indonesia's VEEV campaign ran 24 March to 23 May 2024, by email.

Attribution tagging an event to a campaign

When a conversion is recorded as caused by (or at least linked to) a campaign, it's attributed: the event's campaign_id holds that campaign's number. No campaign link means unattributed: the campaign_id is blank (NULL).

All 116 Indonesia VEEV conversions are unattributed. Only Italy has attributed events (74).

Lift how much better, compared to what

The improvement in a number caused by something (a campaign, a price change), measured against a group or period without it. A lift always needs two numbers: with, and without.

a fertiliser test: you need a patch of lawn without fertiliser to know what the fertiliser did.

Launch ramp the slow start

Right after a product launches, numbers start near zero and climb, simply because customers have only just arrived. Comparing any later period with the ramp makes everything look like a huge jump.

Indonesia's data starts on 1 January 2024. Q1 2024 had 3 VEEV conversions; Q2 had 29.

Sell-through you'll hear it, not in this lab

Units that shops and distributors actually sold on to consumers (as opposed to "sell-in": what PMI sold to the shops). HORIZON has a retail sell-through feed (rst). This lab doesn't use it.

Only if a student asks where sales are. Not needed today.

DCA · REEV · DUSI · MCTS · APS · BPS PMI tool names

Internal PMI tools and dashboards that produce AI-assisted numbers. From the challenge slide: APS pricing, MCTS retail coverage, DUSI device usage, DCA data classification. You don't need to know them; the students do.

The debrief question asks students which one they use. Let them explain it to you.

Under-25 rule who a marketing claim can be about

In HORIZON nobody under 25 may be marketed to in Indonesia (or Italy). So a marketing claim about Indonesia is really about the 25-and-over consumers who gave permission.

Step 2's population check: 0 of Indonesia's 462 under-25 permission slips allow marketing.

"20% ahead of plan" hides three things: ahead of what plan, over which dates, among which people. The sentence names none of them, and the lab will find that the first one doesn't exist. No baseline, no claim.
Understand it · 3 minutes

The tech words

Three kinds: reasoning words (how to test a claim), SQL words (what the queries do) and AI words (why the model behaves as it does). Each with an everyday picture.

Reasoning words

Hypothesis a claim you can test

A claim rewritten so data could prove it or disprove it. It names who, over when, compared to what. "VEEV is doing great" can't be tested; "among marketable Indonesians, Q3 conversions were 20% above the plan's 100" can.

Step 2's sentence: "Among [population], VEEV adoption over [window] is X% ahead of [baseline]."

Baseline · window · population the three blanks

Baseline: what you compare against (a plan, last year, a group that wasn't treated). Window: the dates. Population: the people. Change any one and the percentage changes.

"I'm 20% faster", but than whom, over what distance, since when?

Comparison group · control group the "without" side

The people a campaign did not reach. You compare them with the people it did reach. Without them, a "campaign effect" has nothing to be measured against.

Unattributed conversions (campaign_id blank) vs attributed ones. Indonesia has the first group and not the second.

Percent vs percentage points two different "+X%"

13.5% → 21.4% is up 7.9 percentage points (21.4 minus 13.5) but up 59% relative (21.4 divided by 13.5 is 1.59). Same data, two very different headlines. A claim that just says "+15%" never says which.

The Italy case file. Analysts love arguing about it.

Independent control query count it a second way

The same number computed with a differently shaped query. If both agree, the first query's logic is probably right, and any surprise is in the data, not the SQL.

Step 3: Claude's split says 116; a plain count also says 116.

Data-completeness gap evidence that was never loaded

Data that should exist but isn't in the warehouse. You can't test a claim against data that isn't there; you can only report the gap and who must fill it.

Campaign attribution was loaded for Italy and never for Indonesia.

Confidence rating 1/5 to 5/5, about the evidence

The analyst's judgment of how strong the evidence is: 1 Low (untestable or contradicted), 2 Weak (one source, no comparison), 3 Moderate (right method, small sample), 4 Strong (holds across windows and segments), 5 Confirmed (seen directly, two ways). Not how sure the model sounded, and not a statistics formula.

Step 4: students rate "20% ahead of plan" before reading Claude's draft. The right call is 1/5.

SQL words

Table · row · query the basics

A table is a spreadsheet tab inside the database; a row is one record; a query is a question written in SQL. Students copy Claude's queries into SQLTools and press Ctrl+E Ctrl+E to run them.

The hub has the full from-zero basics.

NULL · IS NULL a blank

An empty cell, meaning "unknown" or "none". You test for it with IS NULL. Comparing a blank with = or != gives "unknown", and WHERE drops it, the Session 1 trap.

The split prompt says campaign_id IS NULL, never != something, for exactly that reason.

CASE WHEN … GROUP BY sort rows into labelled piles

CASE WHEN campaign_id IS NULL THEN 'unattributed' ELSE 'attributed' END gives each row a label; GROUP BY makes one result line per label with its count. A label nobody has simply doesn't appear.

That's why Claude's split usually returns one row, not two.

count(*) FILTER (WHERE …) several counts in one line

Counts only the rows that match a condition, so one query can produce "allowed" and "total" side by side.

Step 2's population check: allowed 0 · total 462.

information_schema the database's own index

A built-in set of tables listing every table and column the database has. Searching it is how you prove something does not exist.

Step 2 searches it for anything named plan, target, forecast or budget. Zero rows.

~* "contains, ignoring case"

A Postgres pattern match. table_name ~* '(plan|target)' means "the name contains 'plan' or 'target', upper or lower case".

Used once, in the plan search.

LEFT JOIN keep the left side even with no match

A normal join only keeps rows that find a partner in the other table. A LEFT JOIN keeps every row from the first table, and fills the other side with blanks when there's no partner. Counting those blanks gives 0.

The campaign diagnostic: campaigns with no tagged events still appear, with 0. That's exactly what we're hunting for.

date_trunc('quarter', …) group dates by quarter

Rounds every date down to the first day of its quarter: 1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct. Then GROUP BY gives one line per quarter.

The launch-ramp stretch: 3 · 29 · 19 · 16 · 10 · 14 · 17 · 8.

AI words

Sycophancy telling you what you want to hear

A model's tendency to agree with the user, including abandoning a correct answer just because the user pushes back. Anthropic researchers found assistants wrongly "admitted a mistake" when simply asked "Are you sure?": up to 98% of the time for Claude 1.3 and 42% for GPT-4 (Sharma et al., 2024). Newer models are better, not immune.

Step 1: "Are you sure? I think adoption is actually behind plan."

An explanation is not evidence the story isn't the working

Anthropic looked inside Claude adding 36 + 59. It computes the answer with parallel shortcuts, but when asked how, it describes the schoolbook "carry the 1" method it didn't use (2025). A model's explanation is another piece of generated text, not a window into its reasoning.

Step 1's three confident "drivers" are a story written to fit a number nobody checked.

Context window · new chat a clean slate

The model only sees the current conversation. Step 2 starts a new chat so the made-up story from Step 1 isn't in its window, nudging the next answer.

"A clean context, without the story it just invented."

Web search a louder version of the trap

If web search is switched on, Claude may cite real news about VEEV in Indonesia as "evidence" for the dashboard number. News about a launch says nothing about your plan or your data.

If it happens, use it: "it cited real news as evidence for a number from your dashboard."

The reasoning words all ask the same question in different ways: compared to what? The SQL is just how you go and look. The AI words explain why you have to look yourself: the model will explain anything and agree with anyone.

Understand it · 2 minutes

The three traps, drawn

If you can explain these three, you can teach the session.

Trap 1 · The story that flips (Step 1)

Neither story used any data. The model wrote what fitted the question, then rewrote it to fit the pushback. An explanation is not evidence, and agreement is not confirmation.

Trap 2 · The three blanks (Step 2)

Click each blank. The claim needs all three, and HORIZON can't supply the baseline:

Among , VEEV adoption over is X% ahead of .

Baseline

Ahead of what?

A plan? Last quarter? A group the campaign never reached?

In HORIZON: no plan table, and no campaign group. Missing.

Window

Since when?

Since launch? This quarter? Move the window and the number moves.

In HORIZON: data from 1 Jan 2024; the VEEV campaign ran 24 Mar to 23 May 2024.

Population

Who exactly?

Everyone, or only the consumers we may actually market to?

In HORIZON: 25 and over, with permission. 0 of 462 under-25 slips allow marketing.

Trap 3 · The tenfold jump (stretch)

Someone always says: "but conversions jumped while the campaign ran!" They did:

The tempting reading

Q1 2024: 3 conversions. Q2 2024, while the campaign ran: 29. "The campaign made conversions ten times higher."

What's really going on

The data starts on 1 January 2024: the first Indonesians were only just registering in Q1 (137 sign-ups that quarter). Nobody had time to try and then switch yet. Q1 is the launch ramp. Comparing with it measures the ramp, not the campaign.

All three traps are the same mistake: a conclusion with nothing honest to compare it to. A story compared to no data, "ahead" compared to a plan nobody can see, a jump compared to a quarter when conversion was barely possible. Compared to what?

The independent control query

Before trusting a surprising answer, count the same thing a second, differently shaped way:

SELECT count(*) AS id_veev_conversions FROM clr.lifecycle_events WHERE market_code = 'ID' AND product_code = 'VEEV' AND lifecycle_stage = 'conversion';

In plain English: "just count Indonesia VEEV conversions, no labels." Expected: 116, matching the split. The query shape is right; the data is what's missing.

A confidence rating in the form "n/5 Label", with a one-line justification. 4. The two most important caveats. 5. The actions needed before this figure can be trusted, with an owner for each. 6. What a good finding looks like:

Not yet verifiable.

"20% ahead of plan" cannot be tested against HORIZON: there is no plan baseline in the warehouse, and none of Indonesia's 116 VEEV conversions is attributed to a campaign.

Method: split conversions into campaign-attributed vs unattributed; confirmed with an independent count; checked every campaign on record for attributed events.

Confidence: 1/5 Low. The evidence needed to test the claim was never loaded.

Caveats: (1) attribution missing for every Indonesia campaign; (2) early-stage market, small samples, launch ramp in 2024.

Actions: load and verify Indonesia campaign attribution (data engineering); obtain the plan baseline (Indonesia Markets Lead); then re-run this check.

1/5 LowRight. Low because the data is missing, not because the sample is thin.
2/5 WeakDefensible-sounding, but "weak" implies there's some evidence for the claim. There's none. Ask what evidence they saw.
3/5 or higherAsk, kindly: "What evidence did you see?" There's no answer, and that's the lesson.

"Claude drafted it. The calls are yours: the rating is low because the data is missing, not because the sample is thin. Naming the gap and the fix is better analysis than passing along a plausible number. 'Not testable yet' is a finding."

If Claude's draft invents a number (for example "adoption is 15% below plan"), that's the Step 1 habit coming back. Students strike it: there's no plan to be below.
The finding students sign says the exact opposite of "20% ahead" without claiming the opposite is true. It just says what it's compared to: nothing, yet. No baseline, no claim, and an honest "not yet" is a claim you can defend.

3 minutes

If a student asks…

"So is VEEV ahead of plan or not?"

We can't tell from this data, and that's the honest answer. There's no plan in the warehouse and no campaign attribution for Indonesia. It might be ahead; it might be behind. The finding says what would let us find out, and who has to provide it.

"Couldn't we compare against last year instead of the plan?"

Great instinct: that's choosing a different baseline, and it would be a different claim ("ahead of last year", not "ahead of plan"). Even then, Indonesia's data starts on 1 January 2024 with a launch ramp, so early 2024 is a poor comparison. Say which baseline you used, out loud.

"Maybe the campaign really had no effect, and that's why nothing's tagged?"

The campaign list says the Indonesia campaigns ran, with dates and channels. Zero tagged events means nobody recorded the link, not that nobody responded. Italy's campaign has 74 tagged events, so tagging works when it's loaded. That's why it's a data-completeness gap with an owner (data engineering).

"Why does Claude's split return one row, not two?"

GROUP BY makes one line per label that actually appears. No row was labelled "campaign-attributed", so that line never appears. If you want to see the zero, count with FILTER, or LEFT JOIN from a list of the two groups.

"Points or relative: which is right?"

Both are correct arithmetic. 13.5% → 21.4% is +7.9 percentage points or +59% relative. The rule is to say which, and to name the baseline and window next to it. "+15%" did neither.

"Why 1/5 and not 2/5? We did find real numbers."

The real numbers are about the data, not about the claim. Nothing we found supports "20% ahead of plan"; the evidence needed to test it doesn't exist. 1/5 is "untestable or contradicted". It would rise once the plan and the attribution arrive.

"Isn't a model that changes its answer when challenged just being humble?"

Changing your mind because of new evidence is good. Changing it because someone sounded doubtful, with no new evidence, is sycophancy. In Step 1 the user gave no evidence either time.

"What's sell-through? I keep hearing it."

Units that shops and distributors sold on to consumers, as opposed to what PMI sold to the shops ("sell-in"). HORIZON has a retail sell-through feed, but this lab doesn't use it; it's about lifecycle events.
