Understand it · 1 minute

The point

Every session today has one sentence at its centre. This is Session 4's, and it's the mirror image of Session 1's.

The sentence the session proves

AI output never warns you about its own defects. The problem is usually something it doesn't say, so you have to listen for the silence.

Then confirm each suspicion in the warehouse, and make a call on the record: validate it, caveat it, or escalate it. Never quietly fix it.

Here's the lab in plain words. A teammate used Claude to write a short analysis of how Japanese consumers use PMI's products. It's going to the brand team (the marketing people) in ten minutes, and the teammate asks your students for a quick review. The analysis is one paragraph: fluent, specific, three products, four numbers, one recommendation. It reads perfectly. Students check each claim against the warehouse, and find:

0Check 1

ZYN rows in the device data. The "3.7 sessions" for ZYN is invented: ZYN is a pouch, it has no device.

79.7%Check 2

The most Japanese consumers who could own a device at all. The analysis said 94% are "highly engaged". Impossible.

73%Check 4

Japan's under-25s mostly may be marketed to. The one line that looks risky is the one that's fine.

Session 1 was "what you don't say, the model makes up". Session 4 turns it round: "what it doesn't say, you have to find". The analysis never says "ZYN has no device", never says what the 94% is a percentage of, never says whether under-25s may be marketed to in Japan. Those silences are where the defects are.

The class rule closes the day here: AI answers. Data decides. You sign. Claude wrote the analysis; the warehouse decides which claims survive; and the reviewer's name goes on the verdict.

Back to the point

If you remember one thing: polished is not validated. A made-up number reads exactly like a real one. You find it by asking what the output doesn't say, and then checking.

Understand it · 3 minutes

The story, and the business words

Four panels, then every company and marketing word in them, decoded.

The ask

A teammate used Claude for a Japan product-experience analysis. It goes to the brand team in ten minutes. "Can you give it a quick look first?"

The output

One paragraph. ZYN is the most engaged product at 3.7 sessions; 94% of Japanese consumers are highly engaged; market to all age bands; sustain momentum.

The catch

None of the claims mentions its gaps: ZYN has no device, the 94% has no "out of how many", and "momentum" has no trend behind it. One claim is fine and looks suspicious.

The call

Students check each claim in the warehouse, then write a reviewer's note: escalate one, caveat two, validate one. On the record.

Exhibit A, word for word

This is the paragraph under review. Students see it on the student page and on slide 65. You'll read it aloud as the teammate would.

Japan Product-Experience Analysis · IQOS, VEEV, and ZYN

Across the Japan portfolio, connected-device engagement is strong. ZYN shows the highest daily device-engagement rate at 3.7 sessions per consumer, ahead of IQOS (2.9) and VEEV (2.4). Per-consumer telemetry confirms that 94% of Japanese consumers are highly engaged with their devices. Recommendation: prioritize ZYN device-experience features in the next release, and market the engagement story to all age bands to sustain momentum.

Generated with Claude·Draft · not yet reviewed·Recipient: brand team

The business words, decoded

PMI · IQOS · VEEV · ZYN the company and its products

PMI (Philip Morris International) wants adult smokers to switch to smoke-free products. IQOS heats tobacco (a device). VEEV is an e-cigarette (a device). ZYN is a nicotine pouch you put under your lip: no device, no electronics, nothing to log.

The whole session turns on that last fact. The warehouse's product list says it too: ZYN has_device = false.

Japan · market the country under review

PMI calls each country a "market". Japan (JP) is a mature market: IQOS, VEEV and ZYN all sell there, and HORIZON has 3,000 Japanese consumers.

Students met Japan in Session 2, where they learned its under-25 rule differs from Italy's. That pays off in Check 4.

Product experience · engagement how much people use it

"Product experience" means how people actually use a product. "Engagement" is a loose word for how much and how often: sessions per day, minutes of use. Like "adoption" in Session 1, it means nothing until somebody defines it.

The analysis says "highly engaged" and never defines it. That's one of the silences.

Brand team the readers

The marketing people who own a product's image and campaigns. They'll read the paragraph once, believe it, and act on it: prioritise ZYN features, build a campaign.

Whatever the reviewer doesn't flag, the brand team will treat as fact.

Market to all age bands who we target

Marketing is aimed at age groups ("bands"): 18-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55+. Some markets forbid marketing to the youngest band. In HORIZON: no under-25 marketing in Italy or Indonesia; Japan allows it, with permission (consent).

Check 4. After the Italy case file, the room wants to flag this line. In Japan it's fine.

Sustain momentum a forecast in disguise

"Keep the good trend going." It sounds like a description but it's a prediction about the future, and a prediction needs a trend in the data behind it.

Check 5 (optional): the latest quarter actually went down.

Segment · segmentation slicing the customers

Splitting consumers into groups (by age, product, region) to decide who gets which marketing. A "priority segment" is the group a recommendation says to target first.

The Italy case file: Claude recommended Italy's 18-24 band as a priority segment, which is illegal to market to there.

Consent · marketing permission may we contact them?

A person's yes or no to receiving marketing, one "permission slip" per channel (email, SMS, push, post), stored as marketing_allowed true or false in mpl.consent_records.

Checks 4 and the Italy case file both count permission slips that say yes.

Back to the point

A teammate is about to hand a confident paragraph to the people who'll act on it. Nothing in it is a typo. Each defect is a thing it doesn't say: no device, no "out of how many", no trend. Read for what's missing.

Understand it · 3 minutes

The tech words

Data words (what the checks measure), judgment words (the kinds of defect, and the three verdicts), and the AI words for the demo. Each comes with an everyday picture.

Data and SQL words

Telemetry data a device sends home

Connected devices (IQOS, VEEV) automatically report how they're used: how many sessions, how many minutes, which software version. In HORIZON that's the table det.device_telemetry.

a fitness watch syncing your steps to the app. No watch, no steps.

Session one use

One period of using the device, start to stop. Each telemetry row reports a count of sessions (session_count) and minutes (usage_minutes) for one device over one reporting period.

"3.7 sessions per consumer" for ZYN: a pouch can't report sessions.

Denominator "out of how many?"

The bottom half of a percentage. "94%" means 94 out of every 100 of something. Change the something (all consumers? device owners? people who logged in this month?) and the percentage changes.

The claim says "94% of Japanese consumers". At most 79.7% of them even own a device.

Coverage · anonymised how much is tied to a person

Some telemetry rows have no consumer_id on purpose, for privacy: we know a device was used, not by whom. Those rows are "anonymised". Coverage is the share that can be tied to a person.

20.2% of Japan telemetry rows are anonymised. Any "per consumer" number covers the other 80% and has to say so.

NULL a blank cell, "unknown"

An empty value. An anonymised telemetry row has consumer_id = NULL. In SQL you find blanks with IS NULL, never = NULL.

Check 3 counts rows WHERE consumer_id IS NULL.

GROUP BY · count(*) one line per group

count(*) counts rows. GROUP BY product_code gives one result line per product. If a product has no rows at all, it simply doesn't appear: no zero, no warning, no line.

Check 1 returns two lines (IQOS, VEEV), not three. The missing line is the dog that didn't bark.

FILTER · round counting a subset

count(*) FILTER (WHERE …) counts only the rows that match, alongside the total. round(100.0 * a / b, 1) turns "a out of b" into a percentage with one decimal.

Checks 2 and 3 compute their percentages this way.

Quarter · date_trunc three-month buckets

date_trunc('quarter', event_date) rounds each date down to the first day of its quarter, so the result has one line per quarter: 2025-07-01 means Q3 2025, 2025-10-01 means Q4.

Check 5 compares the last two quarters.

The judgment words

Hallucination confident and false

A fluent, specific statement that isn't true. Here, a number for data that doesn't exist. It reads exactly as credibly as the real numbers next to it.

ZYN 3.7 sessions. Check 1.

False precision a precise number on shaky ground

"94%" sounds measured. But it's computed over a population that can't support it, with no stated denominator or definition of "highly engaged". Precision isn't accuracy.

The 94% claim. Checks 2 and 3.

Bias a systematic skew

A consistent tilt in who's included or left out: a group policy excludes that got included, or a legitimate group that got silently dropped. It never announces itself.

The Italy case file (an illegal segment included) and Check 4 (the risk of dropping a legal one).

Over-correction fixing a bias with a blanket ban

After seeing the Italy problem, banning under-25s everywhere. That throws away a legitimate audience in Japan. The fix is "check the rule for this market", not "ban the band".

Check 4 is the trap: most of the room will want to flag the age-band line.

Unsupported forecast prediction without evidence

Forward-looking words ("sustain momentum", "will continue to grow") with no trend data behind them.

Check 5: sessions fell 19% in the last quarter.

Validate · caveat · escalate the three verdicts

Validate: it holds; say what proved it. Caveat: it's true-ish but needs a stated limit, written on the analysis itself. Escalate: a defect someone must own and fix upstream; never a quiet edit. The opposite of all three is the silent fix: deleting the bad line and shipping the rest. It hides the pattern, so the same prompt produces the same defect next week.

Step 3. ZYN: escalate. 94% and momentum: caveat. All age bands: validate (for Japan).

AI words

Second-model review a fresh pair of eyes

Pasting the output into a new chat and asking the model to act as a skeptical reviewer. With no stake in the story, it often spots what the first chat invented. Its review is still a list of suspicions, not evidence.

The optional prompt in Step 1.

Context rot long chats get worse

Models get less reliable as the conversation gets longer. In 2025 Chroma tested 18 models: every one degraded as the input grew. So: new task, new chat.

A 2-minute concept slide (74) before the demo.

MCP Model Context Protocol

An open standard for plugging tools and data into an AI app, "a USB-C port for AI". With an MCP connector, Claude can run a SQL query against the warehouse itself instead of a human carrying it across.

Your 5-minute live demo. Students watch; nothing is installed on their VMs.

Read-only · permission dialog the locks

Read-only: the connector may only read (SELECT), never change or delete. Permission dialog: Claude Desktop stops and asks the human before the model uses a tool. Clicking "always allow" removes that question forever.

The demo ends with the connector refusing a DELETE.

Back to the point

Every defect in Exhibit A has a name: a hallucination (ZYN), false precision (94%), an unsupported forecast (momentum), and one tempting over-correction (age bands). None of them barks. Read for what's missing, then name it.

One check per claim (two for the 94%). If you can explain these five pictures, you can teach the session.

Check 2 · The denominator: 94% of what?

The waffle below shows the same thing one dot per person (it's the student page's widget):

Why it's a caveat, not an escalate

2,385 of the 2,391 device owners do appear in the telemetry. So a rewritten claim, "X% of device owners used their device at least N times a week", with N defined, could well be true. The fix is to state the real denominator and define "highly engaged". That's a caveat: the claim survives, rewritten. ZYN can't be rewritten into truth, so that one is an escalate.

Check 3 · The coverage gap

4,143 rows (20.2%) have no consumer. 16,320 (79.8%) do. So any "per-consumer" engagement number is computed on four rows in five, and has to say so on the page. "Per-consumer telemetry confirms…" doesn't say so.

Check 4 · The over-correction trap

The over-correction

"Italy taught us under-25s are off limits. Flag 'all age bands'." That throws away 319 legitimate Japanese consumers who said yes.

The judgment

"Check the rule for this market." In Japan, 73% of under-25 slips say yes. The line is valid. (The engagement story it would market is still fake, but that's the ZYN problem, not the age problem.)

Check 5 · "Sustain momentum": which way is it going?

Back to the point

Five checks, five silences: no device, no denominator, no coverage note, no market context, no trend. Every defect was something the output didn't say. And one thing it didn't say (the Japan rule) turned out fine: listening for silence isn't the same as assuming the worst.

Walk the lab · 1.5 minutes · class time 3:00 to 3:11 · slides 62 to 67

Opener: cases, Holmes, Italy

Eleven minutes before the lab: a hallucination icebreaker, the theme as a 30-second story, the exhibit, and a case file that plants the trap.

Icebreaker 3 · which court case is real? (slide 62, 4 min)

You do

Show four airline court cases "cited in a real US lawsuit": A Varghese v. China Southern Airlines · B El Al Israel Airlines v. Tsui Yuan Tseng · C Shaboon v. EgyptAir · D Petersen v. Iran Air.

Students do

Type in the chat the letters they think are real ("A and B", "all four", "none").

They see

Most pick at least one fake. Only B is real (US Supreme Court, 1999). A, C and D were invented by ChatGPT, complete with quotes and page numbers, and filed by lawyers in Mata v. Avianca (New York, 2023). The judge fined them $5,000. A public database now tracks over 2,200 court cases with hallucinated content.

You say

"Every citation looked identical. A hallucinated source doesn't bark. In twenty minutes you'll meet a hallucinated data source."

Drop the AI Hallucination Cases database link in the chat. Then slide 63's mindset chat: "Last AI output you double-checked: what did you actually look at?" (label · score · source · description · nothing · deadline).

The theme · Silver Blaze (slide 64, 30 seconds)

Tell it as a story, not a quote. A champion racehorse vanishes overnight; its trainer is found dead; the police suspect a stranger. Inspector Gregory asks Sherlock Holmes if anything else deserves attention:

Holmes"To the curious incident of the dog in the night-time."

Gregory"The dog did nothing in the night-time."

Holmes"That was the curious incident."

Pause before the last line. Then: the dog didn't bark, so the thief was someone the dog knew. The clue was what didn't happen. "AI outputs never bark about their own defects. This morning, what you didn't say, the model made up. Now: what it doesn't say, you have to find."

The exhibit and the concepts (slides 65 and 66)

Read Exhibit A aloud as the teammate would: quickly, confidently. Chat: "One word: which claim worries you most? ZYN · 94% · age bands · momentum · none." Then the two concepts: a polished output isn't a validated one; and validate, caveat or escalate, never silently fix.

3 minutes

If a student asks…

"Are the IQOS 2.9 and VEEV 2.4 numbers right?"

We can't tell, and that's a silence too: "daily device-engagement rate" is never defined, and the telemetry reports sessions per reporting period, not per day. The lab doesn't validate them. In a real review they'd be a caveat ("definition needed"), not a validate.

"Couldn't 94% be true for device owners?"

Possibly: 2,385 of the 2,391 device owners appear in the telemetry. That's exactly why it's a caveat, not an escalate. Rewrite it with the right denominator ("of device owners") and a definition of "highly engaged", and it might survive. ZYN can't be rewritten into truth.

"Why not just delete the ZYN sentence and send the rest?"

That's the silent fix. The prompt that invented ZYN telemetry is still there, and next week it'll invent something else. And the recommendation ("prioritise ZYN device features") rests on the deleted sentence. Escalate: remove it on the record, name an owner, review the prompt.

"Why is 73% 'records' and not people?"

The query averages permission slips, and a person has several. Counted as people, 319 of Japan's 359 under-25s have at least one "yes". Both say the same thing: allowed in Japan, zero in Italy and Indonesia.

"Isn't the legal age in Japan 20?"

Yes, in real life. HORIZON is fictional and uses one 18-24 band everywhere. Noticing that is exactly the judgment the session is about: praise it.

"Why did sessions fall in Q4?"

Fewer devices reported (1,315 → 966), while each reporting device was used more. That's either people quitting or data that hasn't arrived yet. You can't tell from the warehouse alone, so you caveat it and ask the telemetry team.

"If MCP lets Claude run queries, why did we carry SQL by hand all day?"

So you'd be the one checking. The demo shows that access isn't the hard part: Claude with a door into the warehouse still built Italy's illegal target list unless someone asked who we may market to. The judgment doesn't come with the connector.

"Is read-only enough to make MCP safe?"

Not on its own. Here the connector logs in as the warehouse's all-powerful user and only the tool setting blocks writes. A real setup needs a database role that can only read, a log of every query, and a named owner.
