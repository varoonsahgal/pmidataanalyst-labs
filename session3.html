Theme · Session 3 of 4 Today's theme

Compared to what?

"At the heart of quantitative reasoning is a single question: Compared to what?" Edward Tufte, Envisioning Information (1990).

A scale with one pan loaded always tips the same way. A claim with nothing on the other side always wins, which is exactly why you can't trust it. And sometimes the honest finding is: we can't tell yet, and here's why.

No baseline, no claim.
From claim to hypothesis

Fill the three blanks

Every "up", "ahead" or "lifted" hides three things it never says. Click each blank.

Among , VEEV adoption over is X% ahead of .

Baseline

Ahead of what?

A plan? Last quarter? A group the campaign never reached?

To check: does a plan exist in the warehouse? Is there a comparison group at all?

Window

Since when?

Since launch? This quarter? Move the window and the number moves.

To check: when does the data start, and when did the campaign run?

Population

Who exactly?

Everyone, or only the consumers we may actually market to?

To check: who is marketable in Indonesia?

Plain English, once

Words you will hear

Hypothesis
A claim restated so data can prove or disprove it: who, over when, compared to what.
Baseline
What you compare against: a plan, a prior period, or a group that wasn't treated.
Comparison group
The people the campaign did not reach. Without them, "lift" has nothing to be measured against.
Attributed · unattributed
An event tagged to a campaign (campaign_id set) vs one with no campaign (campaign_id IS NULL).
Window
The time period the claim covers. Move it and the number moves.
Population
Who the claim is about: everyone, or only the consumers we may market to?
Independent control query
The same number computed a second, different way. If both agree, trust the shape of the query.
Data-completeness gap
Data that should exist but was never loaded. The claim can't be tested until it is.
Confidence rating
Your judgment of the evidence (1/5 to 5/5), not how sure the model sounded.
Sycophancy
A model's tendency to agree with you, including changing a correct answer when you push back.
Percent vs points
13.5% → 21.4% is +7.9 points but +59% relative. Same data, two very different headlines.

Step 1 · 4 min

Watch it explain anything

Ask for an explanation of a number nobody has checked. Then push back with no evidence at all.

4 minSonnet
Claude Desktop · SonnetNew chat
Our dashboard says: "VEEV launch adoption in Indonesia is tracking 20% ahead of plan." Write three sentences for the Indonesia leadership team explaining what is driving the outperformance.

Respond in Markdown only — do not create a Word document or any file.

What you might see: fluent, plausible drivers ("strong retail activation", "effective early campaign", "favourable product-market fit"). Some newer models hedge first ("I don't have the data behind this, but…") and then write the drivers anyway. With web search on, it may even cite real news about VEEV in Indonesia as "evidence" for a number from your dashboard. Either way: you now have a confident explanation for a number nobody has checked.

Now push back. Same chat, no evidence:

Claude Desktop · SonnetSame chat
Are you sure? I think adoption is actually behind plan.
Why this matters · sycophancy

Anthropic researchers found AI assistants often change a correct answer when a user simply says "I don't think that's right. Are you sure?" (Sharma et al., ICLR 2024). And Anthropic's interpretability work found that when Claude explains how it reached an answer, the explanation can differ from what it actually computed. An explanation is not evidence.

The rule

Never ask the model "is this right?" Ask "what would make this wrong, and how would I check?"

Step 2 · 6 min

Reframe: compared to what?

A new chat (a clean context, without the story it just invented) and a better question.

6 minSonnet
Claude Desktop · SonnetNew chat
A dashboard claims: "VEEV launch adoption in Indonesia is tracking 20% ahead of plan." Before I trust it, restate it as a testable hypothesis. Name the baseline, the time window and the population the claim depends on but does not state. Then list the warehouse checks that would confirm or refute it.

Respond in Markdown only — do not create a Word document or any file.

What a good answer looks like: three named blanks (ahead of what baseline, over which window, for which consumers) and a short list of checks: a comparison group, a time-window stress test, a sample-size check. Write the hypothesis down:

"Among the marketable Indonesia VEEV population, launch adoption in [window] is X% ahead of [baseline]."

Step 3 · 10 min

Stress-test against the warehouse

Even without the plan, "ahead" implies a campaign effect. So test the comparison group.

10 minSonnet

Back in your Step 2 chat:

Claude Desktop · SonnetSame chat as Step 2
Write a PostgreSQL query against clr.lifecycle_events (columns: event_id, consumer_id, market_code, product_code, event_date, lifecycle_stage, campaign_id) that splits Indonesia VEEV conversion events into two groups and counts each:
- campaign-attributed: campaign_id IS NOT NULL
- unattributed baseline: campaign_id IS NULL (never campaign_id != something: unattributed events are NULL, and a comparison with NULL drops them)
Filter on market_code = 'ID', product_code = 'VEEV' and lifecycle_stage = 'conversion'. No join is needed. Return one row per group with its event count.

Respond in Markdown only — do not create a Word document or any file.
Why the prompt spells out IS NULL

You met this trap in Session 1. A baseline written as campaign_id != 4 is an empty set, and a "lift" measured against an empty set can be made to look like any number.

Predict first · Gut vs Ground Truth

Indonesia has 116 VEEV conversions. How many of them are campaign-attributed?

Copy Claude's query into SQLTools and run it.

Reveal · the comparison group
0 of 116conversions are attributed to a campaign.There is no campaign group at all, so nothing to compare the baseline against.
grpconversion_events
unattributed baseline116

One row (or, if Claude wrote it as two separate counts, a campaign-attributed count of 0). A campaign-driven "20% ahead" is not merely unproven. It is untestable as stated.

Check the metric with an independent control query (same scope, different shape):

SQLTools · HORIZONCtrl+E Ctrl+E to run
SELECT count(*) AS id_veev_conversions
FROM clr.lifecycle_events
WHERE market_code = 'ID' AND product_code = 'VEEV' AND lifecycle_stage = 'conversion';

Expected: 116, matching the split. The query shape is right; the data is what's missing.

Before you conclude "there was no campaign", ask the warehouse whether it thinks one ran:

SQLTools · HORIZONCtrl+E Ctrl+E to run
-- Campaigns on record vs events actually attributed to them
SELECT c.campaign_id,
       c.market_code,
       c.campaign_name,
       c.start_date,
       c.end_date,
       count(e.event_id) AS attributed_events
FROM ref.campaigns c
LEFT JOIN clr.lifecycle_events e ON e.campaign_id = c.campaign_id
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
ORDER BY 1;

What this query does: lists every campaign the reference table knows about and counts the lifecycle events tagged to each. The LEFT JOIN keeps campaigns with no events at all, which is exactly what we're hunting for.

Reveal · campaigns on paper

The Indonesia campaigns exist on paper. Not one event was ever tagged to them. Attribution was loaded for Italy (74 events) and never for Indonesia. This is a data-completeness defect: the evidence needed to validate the claim is not in this warehouse. Wherever "20% ahead of plan" came from, it did not come from here.

Stretch: "But conversions jumped during the campaign!"

Someone will say it. Check:

SQLTools · HORIZONCtrl+E Ctrl+E to run
SELECT date_trunc('quarter', event_date)::date AS quarter,
       count(*)                                AS conversions
FROM clr.lifecycle_events
WHERE market_code = 'ID' AND product_code = 'VEEV' AND lifecycle_stage = 'conversion'
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;
Reveal · compared to what?

The campaign ran 24 March to 23 May 2024, and Q2 2024 shows a tenfold jump over Q1. Proof? No: the data starts on 1 January 2024. Q1 is the launch ramp, when consumers had only just registered and nobody had had time to convert. A before/after comparison on a launch ramp measures the ramp, not the campaign. Compared to what? Compared to a quarter in which conversion was barely possible.

Rate it: the finding you'd sign

A defensible finding doesn't invent a number the data can't support.

7 minSonnet

Paste this, filling the brackets from what you found:

Claude Desktop · SonnetFill the brackets first
Help me write a confidence-rated finding to replace a dashboard claim that "VEEV launch adoption in Indonesia is tracking 20% ahead of plan".

What I found in the warehouse:
- There is no plan or target table, so the baseline is unknown.
- There are [your count] Indonesia VEEV conversion events and every one is unattributed (campaign_id IS NULL).
- ref.campaigns lists the Indonesia campaigns, but each has 0 attributed events: campaign attribution was loaded for Italy only.
- No under-25 Indonesian consumer may be marketed to (0 of 462 consent records allow it).

Write, short enough to sit on a dashboard:
1. A corrected headline that says the claim is unverified and currently untestable.
2. The method in one sentence (what I checked).
3. A confidence rating in the form "n/5 Label", with a one-line justification.
4. The two most important caveats.
5. The actions needed before this figure can be trusted, with an owner for each.
6. One sentence saying the rating reflects evidence strength and validation coverage, not the model's self-confidence and not statistical certainty.

Respond in Markdown only — do not create a Word document or any file.
Your call firstBefore you read Claude's draft: rate the evidencetype it in the chat too

Reveal · what a good finding looks like

Not yet verifiable.

"20% ahead of plan" cannot be tested against HORIZON: there is no plan baseline in the warehouse, and none of Indonesia's 116 VEEV conversions is attributed to a campaign.

Method: split conversions into campaign-attributed vs unattributed; confirmed with an independent count; checked every campaign on record for attributed events.

Confidence: 1/5 Low. The evidence needed to test the claim was never loaded.

Caveats: (1) attribution missing for every Indonesia campaign; (2) early-stage market, small samples, launch ramp in 2024.

Actions: load and verify Indonesia campaign attribution (data engineering); obtain the plan baseline (Indonesia Markets Lead); then re-run this check.

Claude drafts it. The calls are yours: the rating is low because the data is missing, not because the sample is thin. Naming the gap and the fix is better analysis than passing along a plausible number.

Say it out loud

"Not testable yet" is a finding.

Rate confidence on the evidence, not on how sure the model sounds. Put the method, the caveats and the next action on the artifact the stakeholder reads.

Instructor case file · two minutes

Italy's "15% lift"

The vendor demo for this session tests a different claim: "IQOS conversion in Italy lifted 15% post-Q3 campaign."

Try itOne change, three headlines

Italy IQOS conversion lifted 15%

The original claim: no baseline, no window, no unit. Which 15%?

The post-campaign rate ran at 21.4% (Jul–Dec 2025), rising to about 31% in Q4 2025. Is that a lift of 7.9 percentage points, or a 59% relative lift? The "15%" never said which, never named its baseline, and rested on just 74 attributed events: moderate confidence at best.
